What Changed

The key architectural change is replacing LLM-based chunk grading (reasoning) with similarity-based dropoff sampling. Instead of having gpt-5-mini evaluate each chunk's relevance, V2 uses embedding similarity scores directly with a dropoff threshold to decide when to switch to the next community. This is faster and cheaper (no per-chunk LLM calls) but relies entirely on embedding similarity rather than semantic understanding.

5. Analysis: Impact of Removing Reasoning

Local Questions: Improved (+2.0%)

Dropoff sampling slightly improved local question performance. For fact-finding questions targeting specific passages, the similarity-based approach is sufficient — the most relevant chunks are typically the nearest neighbors, and the LLM grading step in V1 may have occasionally filtered out relevant chunks that scored below the relevance threshold of 2.

Global Questions: Degraded (−4.3%)

The larger drop in global question performance suggests that reasoning-based chunk grading provided value for cross-document synthesis. The LLM grader could assess whether a chunk contributed new topical coverage, while pure similarity-based dropoff may cluster around a few dominant themes and miss peripheral-but-relevant topics.

Cost/Latency Tradeoff

V2 eliminates per-chunk LLM calls (up to 1,200 × gpt-5-mini calls per question), significantly reducing cost and latency. The ~10s reduction in average response time (70s → 60s) is modest, but the cost savings from removing the reasoning LLM are substantial for high-volume deployments.

7. Summary

GraphRAG Zero V2 (dropoff sampling, no reasoning) shows a mixed impact from removing the reasoning pipeline:

  • Local questions improved to 85.7% (best across all systems) — pure similarity retrieval is effective for document-specific fact-finding
  • Global questions degraded to 61.2% — the loss of LLM-based chunk grading reduces thematic diversity in retrieved context
  • Overall pass rate dropped slightly from 74.6% to 73.4% — the global degradation outweighs the local improvement
  • LGR remains the overall leader at 75.4%, primarily due to its superior global question handling (71.4%)

Recommendation: The choice between V1 and V2 depends on the deployment context. For applications dominated by local/specific queries, V2 (dropoff) offers comparable or better quality at lower cost. For applications requiring broad thematic synthesis, V1 (reasoning) or LGR are preferred.
